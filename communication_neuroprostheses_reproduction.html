1. Hardware Memory Wall (Fig. 2g, h)300 GB → 19.6 GB RAM
Composing a 125k-word pronunciation dictionary L with a 5-gram language model G into TLG.fst = T ∘ min(det(L ∘ G)) inflates host RAM to 300 GB (and second-pass LLM rescoring provisions 48–80 GB VRAM). We fit the entire stack on one consumer PC (64 GB RAM, 1× RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB) via on-demand mmap + .idx WFST state tables (19.6 GB RAM) or KenLM tries (1.46 GB RAM) and decoupled FP16/4-bit LLM rescoring (13.0 GB VRAM).
2. Acoustic Phase Transition (Fig. 2i)PER < 25% Gate
Because English words average 3.4 phonemes (WER_no-LM = 1 − (1 − PER)^3.4), the CTC-TLG + LLM cascade beats raw phoneme error (WER < PER) in 79% (45/57) of sessions when greedy PER ≤ 25%, but only 20% (4/20) when PER > 25%. Above 25% PER (and at ~60% PER in sparse inner speech), 125k WFST beam search hallucinates word insertions that even NB=400 OPT/Qwen rescoring cannot rescue.
3. Global Linguistic Ceiling (Fig. 6a–d)~85% Blocked in Tier 3
CTC-TLG is triple-gated by a discrete phoneme set T, a finite written dictionary L, and a text LM/LLM G: tonal and agglutinative languages require Tier 2 subword/tonal redesign, and ~85% of the world’s ~7,000 spoken languages (Tier 3) lack a written lexicon or text LM and are blocked outright. Continuous Brain-to-Voice (Wairagkar et al., 2025, 2026) synthesizes vocal-tract acoustics directly at inference (0% in Tier 3).
